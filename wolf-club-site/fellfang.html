Fellfang

Code of Honor | Witchdoctors | Mysterious

Character Folder Pack Journal Map App Template

Though Fellfang is not ancient to the area, their culture is as old as the odes that grace their land.

In their early days, Fellfangirs were known for their lack of roots and nomadic lifestyle. Preferring the Eastern region, they migrated alongside the prey that sustained them, specifically larger game like elk and caribou that travelled South for winter and North for summer.

The pack was nothing if not benevolent in their travels, often welcomed as traders that brought fortune and good health with each encounter. They became well-known for their nomadic lifestyle, honour-bound culture, mysterious nature-based spirituality, and adept skill sets in botany and medicine. Yet they guarded their secrets with contempt; though they welcomed knowledge from other packs, they showed reluctance in returning the favour.

Eventually, the Fellfangir would vanish as quickly as they had arrived.

While recent times have seen them abandon their nomadic wanderlust, the era is fondly referred to as "The First Ways".

History

The Dark Days

Where their migratory prey was once abundant, a sudden disappearance of these herds saw the pack starved and weak. A resulting lapse in judgment prompted a wolf by the name of Arn to take the lead. Ever the tactician, albeit one with a brutish nature, he hoped to lead the pack the way it had always been: Following the herds, trading what they could, and offering their medicinal talents.

As time progressed and the winter drew near, however, Arn realized that his pack was dying. Shieldwolves grew feeble and hunters could no longer catch larger game. A change was necessary in order to ensure their survival and Arn would be the one to lead the transition.

From thereon, Fellfang's ways radically changed. Gone were the days of peace and benevolence shown to their allies, for the era of marauding and desperate survival began.

Arn's volatile nature ensured the pack's legacy through raids and cruelty, the theft of resources from territories where they were once welcomed, and downing anyone who stood in their way. Education fell to the wayside and its absence bore marauders, pirates, and those who would take far more than they gave to the earth. Arn's rule — strengthened by his greatest fear — cultivated a pack of brutish killers that would continue for generations to come. Adaptation to poor circumstances became their key to survival, no matter the cost.

In Arn's wake came a string of Jarls, all cruel, unstable, and very short-lived. Their grapple with longevity stemmed from the apparent stress of their role and the constant threat of sedition that would slowly drive them to madness or mortal illness. Each Jarl possessed the same temper and bloodlust of their predecessors, an affliction that would later be dubbed "Arn's Curse", for it manifested under great duress in wolves only of his own bloodline.

As time passed, recent years saw the rise of another cruel and unjust Jarl in Fellfang: Story. After his father's assassination and the exile of the alleged murderer — Story's own brother, Fable — he seized the title of Jarl. Although such a succession evoked rumors of foul play, it was only until later that the pack realized the truth of Story's treacherous climb to power.

The time following Fable's banishment from the pack would be counted among the darkest in Fellfang history, for Story's fear of his brother's return inspired a leader far more cruel than any who came before him.

Modern Fellfang

Upon Fable's inevitable return, he brought with him the destruction of the Old Ways.

From the ashes of Story's corpse rose an era of peace and prosperity. Restoring education and old witch-doctor secrets, as well as re-instilling balance and benevolence as core values, brought Fellfang closer to The First Ways than it had been in generations — but with a twist.

No longer adhering to the ancient ways of their nomadic ties, but adapting themselves to curate balance and peace in an attempt to redeem their past actions, the pack settled. Fable went down in history as the Jarl to establish Fellfang its own territory.

Meanwhile, those loyal to Story and the Dark Days were banished from Fellfang, only allowed to return should they renounce their marauding habits.

Yet, not all would be well.

Lady Polaris — Fable's partner — succumbed to an illness that not even the witch-doctors knew how to cure. Despite his strong connection to Fellfang, Fable ultimately decided that he, Polaris, and their daughter would seek out an herbalist in faraway lands.

In his place, his last-born son, Lincoln, took to the throne after the abdication of Fable's first-born.

Though the burden of leadership weighed heavily on young Lincoln's shoulders, he took it in stride. With a clear love for Fellfang and its culture, he successfully broadened their horizons and established alliances long-since forgotten. Under his rule, the pack moved into a Golden Era and the Dark Days became but a whisper in the wind.

But all good things must come to an end.

The ghost wolves arrived upon their shores like fog in the morning. These white-furred enemies with little morals and large fangs tore through Fellfang just as they tore through the entire domain, unrelenting and wreaking havoc wherever they went. Lincoln's best efforts were met with nothing but carnage.

These hostilities culminated in a massive battle later dubbed the Hive Wars. Together, pack-wolves of all allegiances stood against the ghost wolf threat and drove them back. Yet, not without great loss.

Lincoln, the beloved Golden Jarl of Fellfang, met his end in the company of his friends and partner who would later succeed him: Valera.

The death of Jarl Lincoln was a heavy loss. The pack was shaken to its roots and the next few moons were spent in mourning.

Valera took over Lincoln's duties and, though she had formerly promised him to lead with as much peace and prosperity as he did, it was a promise that would not be kept.

The harshness of the war and the traumatic loss of her beloved partner hung over the new Jarl like a dark cloud.

The war was over, and life for the Fellfangir continued on. The arrival of the Jarlsen, the children of the late Jarl Lincoln and Jarl Valera, should have marked the beginning of a brighter future for Fellfang. But rather, it directly preceded Fellfang's descent into a dark era.

Distrust, violence, injustice, and cruelty — each is but a piece of the maelstrom that was Valera's rule. Gone were the days of equal punishment, of familial bonds between non-kin, and of hospitality and honor. Grun were to be met with hostility. Trivial offenses to the Jarlsen were met with harsh and unjust punishment, while the offenses of a select few garnered only minimal judgment. The kidnapping of children was purported as justified.

Each new injustice sparked further discontent, and unrest was brewing within the Skuld…

As her rule had begun with death, so too did it end. Soon after an altercation between two packmates that saw them dead, Jarl Valera, two of her Jarlsen, and her newly appointed Thane disappeared from Fellfang without a word. The only evidence of their departure were stale trails and abandoned camps, but neither could be followed.

It was clear: Jarl Valera did not want to be found.

Fellfang Now

The disappearance of their Jarl, bad enough on its own, was soon followed by a grim omen. A solar eclipse cast a dark shadow upon Fellfang's territory, and the resulting strange behavior of wildlife in their territory was the final sign needed. Their territory, for the time being, was not safe. Many Fellfangir left the pack for good, all of their own reasons, while the whole of Fellfang that remained headed for Xassa where they were given shelter as they decided their next course of action: a vote for who would succeed Valera.

As the heat of summer began to set in, the Fellfangir appointed their newest Jarl: the former Chandorian, Virago.

Foreign Relations

Fellfang wolves are alert and extremely wary of newcomers. They're not used to interacting with other wolves on a friendly basis, and tend to hold individual strangers at a distance. Despite the tension, they actually tend to be more welcoming than most packs. Their Honor also dictates that if a stranger is deemed to not be dangerous, Fellfang is culturally obligated to offer them food and shelter for one night before sending them on their way. However, once a wolf is part of the pack, they are considered family, and are welcomed into all of the loyalty and love that entails. Despite their suspicion towards letting in outsiders, they tend to be extremely amicable towards other packs, and make a strong effort to have good diplomatic relationships with the surrounding territories. They are strong believers in having friends and allies.

Culture

Fellfang often struggles with two identities. The reign of Arn and the Jarls that followed him introduced a violent, almost militant vein into their culture — one they are still currently healing from and attempting to purge from their ranks. Only a single generation has passed since the last of Arn's influence, and though there are some still alive to remember the pirating, Fellfang today strives to maintain balance just as they had during the era of The First Ways.

The First Ways [with capitals] is a general term used to describe everything in relation to their origins. Meaning their nomadic routes, their wanderlust and benevolence — to even their sophisticated healing methods that are renowned across the region.

They rely heavily on their spirituality in which they believe in universal balance. For every action comes a positive or negative reaction and with it, a wolf must strive to maintain said equilibrium.

Similarly, wolves of Fellfang are notoriously known for their honor. Without it, there is no Fellfang just as a pack would sooner be deemed bandits rather than a tight-knit group that rely on one another.

Those within Fellfang are expected to adhere to the customs and conventions surrounding their concept of honor. Despite this however, their honor is not to be mistaken with morality for the two are related but not necessarily interchangeable.

Their code of conduct and idea of honor revolves around that of balance, justice and chivalry. Even if a wolf does not believe in Fellfang's spirituality or has their own system of beliefs upon their admittance: their Code of Honor is the secular contract that all wolves within the Skuld must follow.

Fellfang and Healing

While also being an attainable rank in the pack, a huge part of Fellfang's identity comes from its unusual but extremely effective healers known as Witch-Doctors. Their practices involve healing with herbs found in and around their territory as well as their spirituality. Whether or not the latter makes a difference however is up to personal opinion, but one thing is for certain: whatever it is they are doing, it works.

The Witch-Doctors are well known around the region and more often than not are sought out due to their unusually keen senses in all things medicinal.

Despite their secretive nature, those that carry ailments, illnesses, or wounds are welcomed into the territory far easier than those who otherwise appear healthy. Though no Fellfangir would ever recklessly bring a stranger to the heart of their territory, a temporary residence may be given due to the Witch-Doctors' duty to help those who are incapable of helping themselves.

Witch-Doctors are well versed in the regional herbs as well as those brought from their time in the Western region of the domain. They are cultivated to acclimate to Eastern temperatures and grown in closely guarded groves during the summer.

Similarly, the Witch-Doctors have also come to know more complex things such as setting broken bones and the use of hot springs for consumptive coughs. It is not unheard of for the healers to use barbed thorns to close wounds — a practice that may often receive more upturned brows than not.

The spiritual aspect of their practice includes ritualistic prayers and offers to the spirits; particularly Koma. The use of crystals and other gems within their practice are believed to hold certain properties dependent on that of the stone.

Fellfang and Marauding

The idea or practice of marauding, stealing, attacking or raiding for resources is a bit of a sore subject for Fellfang. While there was once a time they employed all of these things — these practices are outdated and strictly forbidden within Fellfang.

Customs and Events

Rite of Passage

A trial every Serf and adolescent Fellfangir must pass before taking on a task. The trial spans across 2 days and 1 night.

Julentide Festival

A festival celebrated between the months of December, January, and February. This celebration revolves around reinvigorating the world's energy through jubilation, love, and good will.

Solstice Celebrations

Fellfang is very in tune with the seasons as it's all a part of the world's ability to renew and re-balance. Though not a pack-wide event, Fellfang are encouraged to celebrate solstices to honor the progression of time during any real-life solstice.

Burials

Wolves who pass on are mourned at the densite for a day before their bodies are adorned with flowers and sent to sea via the Crystal Run, echoing how the soul is taken to the afterlife. This burial is the only way to prevent a departed spirit from becoming a ghost.

Eldreblot

A memorial celebration during the month of June where Fellfangir pay respects to their ancestors and those who have fallen. The dead are honored through feasting and stories of those passed on.

Ondmuna

During Ondmuna, the spirits, ghosts, and ancestors are thought to be particularly active and the veil between realms is thought to be particularly thin. Fellfang will throw festivals in honor of the spirits, think back on ancestors, and ward themselves against the threat of ghosts/draugr. They will bring down an elk or other large prey in a pack-wide hunt, and feast on it, singing songs, and listening to stories of the spirits told by the pack's Sages. Fellfangir are also prone to enjoying the spookier side of things, telling scary stories, pranking one another, and generally enjoying the mysteries of season as days grow darker.

Svasslager

A Fellfangir delicacy — fermented apples that have developed a kick. Generally reserved for special occasions, primarily during Julentide celebrations.

Beliefs

Though Fellfang accepts all walks of life and their beliefs, those born of Fellfang blood would know that their belief system has more to do with spirituality than that of a specific religion. While they do have their own set of revered deities, it is less about worship and more about the understanding of worldly balance.

Those who were honorable in life may join their ancestors in Ondheim, a paradise or afterlife. Those who were maleficent and brought harm upon individuals around them would be condemned to the underworld to serve out their punishment.

Because of this system they have the innate belief in ghosts, spirits and elemental entities that coexist on a similar cosmic plane. Fellfang see these being not as gods or something to worship and devote loyalty to, but rather closer to allies or enemies on a similar ranking as them.

Furthermore, Fellfangir believe that spirits inhabit the world around them and can reside in anything that is not a creature (which they do believe to have souls of their own in most cases).

Spirits, while not deities or omnipotent, can harness the ambient energy of the earth and use that to fuel their believed powers. Some of which include: illusions, visions, healing, telepathy or appearing in the form of a regional animal. Spirits may also choose to help or hinder wolves and range on a scale from benevolence to maleficence dependent on their own type and that of the individual in question.

Fellfangir will often appeal to spirits for guidance and aid during hard times. Their spirituality is less about its effect on the afterlife and more about an individual's balance in the physical world.

Energy

Energy equals life — the pervading, cosmic connection that ties reality itself together, from the grandest mountain to the tiniest leaf.

Balance

One cannot make something out of nothing. There is no light without shadow, no life without death — every action has an equal reaction.

Draugr / Ghosts

Departed spirits of creatures that have not moved on to the afterlife. Usually considered malevolent, so haunted places are feared and avoided.

Spirits

Incorporeal energy forces present in all living things, distinct from the soul — morally neutral entities Fellfang's witch-doctors pray to for help.

Omens

Signs of things to come, good, bad, or neutral. Sages often read the stars to decipher omens.

Ancestors

Souls who have entered the afterlife but chosen to return and visit or guard someone still living — considered almost entirely benevolent.

Ondheim

The paradise afterlife reserved for the Honorable.

The Underworld

A peaceful limbo for the dead that did not live by the Code of Honor.

Named Spirits

Koma and her Children

Koma is believed to be the spirit most interested in the affairs of mortals. Oftentimes taking the appearance of a golden wolf or dove; she is believed to be good-natured but judicious. She has many children who share the same sentiment towards mortals.

Valda

The Trickster. Valda is thought to appear in many different shapes in order to deceive her victims but prefers the form of a black fox. While not inherently evil, she does not have the best interests of wolves in mind and is often seen manipulating them to get what she wants. Foxes are seen as her allies and are therefore distrusted by Fellfang.

Eir

Taking the form of a white raven, Eir is said to be a spirit who bestows good fortune unto those he shows himself to. Acting as a beacon in hard times, offering luck and good energy when needed. Crows and ravens are thought to be his allies and so Fellfang often welcome their presence within the territory.

Pack Life

With a harsh territory it comes to no surprise that the wolves here have adapted to the weather. Unphased by blizzards in the winter or strong coastal storms in the summer, Fellfangir are hardly deterred by these phenomenons in the slightest.

Having spent the last eight season-cycles in their current territory, the wolves of Fellfang are known for their wiry exteriors, adaptability and were previously renowned for their chivalry in the face of strangers. At first glance they seem rather standoffish and guarded, intimidating with their size and roughened northern coats.

Yet inside the pack between Skuld-mates, Fellfangir are as warm and family-oriented as they come. Often forging bonds with one another through battle and hardships, bonds that may or may not surpass the expectations of blood-relation.

While their origins take them through many eras of violence and piracy, healing and education, Fellfangir at their core have always believed in their Code of Honor. As such, they practice these beliefs through their actions within and outside the pack.

To strangers they may seem cordial, but not always appreciative in recent times — more often than not, they have returned to their guarded habits and keep their secrets close to their chest.

That said, they are not so quick to turn away an unfortunate soul. As secretive as they may seem, they may give haven to those who are in desperate need of it. Though it is often on a case-by-case basis, Fellfangir will more often than not choose to help those in need rather than turn them away.

They are renowned for their healers — the Witch-Doctors — and their practice of medicine, spirituality and custom are all intrinsically linked to the welfare of the individual and of the pack as a whole.

Laws

The Code of Honour

  • Actions have consequences. For every good, there is an equal reward. For every evil, there is an equal consequence. Those who commit crimes will receive equal punishment.
  • Fight fairly. A fight that isn't fair should not be fought.
  • Bystanding. Bearing false witness, stealing and other infractions that hurt the pack may forfeit your membership or be punishable by the Jarl or Thanes.
  • Honesty and accountability. A wolf should be honest and sincere about their intentions. Promises should be kept and owed debts require payment.
  • Duels and disagreements. Disagreements are to be settled by non-lethal duels overseen by the Jarl or Thane. Though declining a duel is allowed, it is considered cowardly.
  • Respecting life. Respect the dignity of all living things. Respect the balance of nature and do nothing to upset it.
  • Protect the Skuld. The Skuld is to be considered family and though some bond relations may be stronger than others, every pack-member deserves courtesy and protection.
  • The Jarl's word. The Jarl's word is binding over all others. Disobeying, conspiring or other infractions may result in banishment, scarification or public humiliation. Disobey at your own risk.

Other Laws

  • Follow the Code Of Honour.
  • Give courtesy to your superior and peers.
  • Taking a Mate. Marrying within the pack is permitted but must be approved by the Jarl. Similarly, if one desires to take a partner from outside the pack, said individual must join Fellfang prior to their oaths. Serfs cannot take mates.
  • Regarding marriage. Marriage is monogamous in Fellfang as they believe a marriage of more than two is unbalanced and therefore negatively impacts the world's energy. Polygamy cannot be practiced, polyamory is discouraged but not punishable.
  • Leaving the territory. It is permitted as long as it does not negatively affect the diplomatic relations with other packs and such an outing must last no longer than 2 days unless approved by the Jarl.
  • Fellfang Trials. Every young adult and Serf must undergo the Coming of Age ceremony (RPing or Picture encouraged but not required).
  • Regarding outsiders. Do not welcome Grun blindly into the territory. They may only enter if they are deemed not a threat by a Drengr or higher and have a good reason for entrance.
  • Crimes. Crimes are punishable by scarification, exile, and in rare cases, death.

Please be advised that wolves who act against Fellfang Laws or the Jarl's word may be inflicted with in-character consequence. Disobey the rules at your own risk.

Tasks

Sage — The keepers of the First Ways.

Sages are known for their memorization of Fellfang's history and culture. They are tasked with reminding Fellfang of their benevolent ways in times of great duress as well as enforcing the laws of Fellfang. Sages have a deep connection to the land and its energy and in doing so are renowned for their ability to read the stars and uncover omens at higher rankings. They contain the ability to track days, months and even years in some cases. Sages are encouraged to take in new Fellfangir to guide them through their assimilation into Fellfang culture. They help to keep up pack morale with songs, stories and poems and in some cases, may act as advisors or counselors to members. It is recommended that Sages be born of Fellfang blood and strongly believe in their customs.

Witch-Doctor — The revered healers of Fellfang.

Since its inception Witch-Doctors have been sought out for their skill and otherworldly ability regarding all things medicinal. Witch-Doctors use a mixture of Fellfang's spirituality and exotic healing methods from the East to heal their patients. They have a deep knowledge of botany and Fellfang's beliefs system and how the two subjects intertwine during the healing process. Unique practices Fellfang witch-doctors specialize include but are not limited to are: complex herb mixtures, extensive knowledge of mushrooms, pressure points to alleviate pain, setting broken bone, the benefits of hotsprings for lung issues and the benefits of salty sea water. Though wolves do not need to be born to become Witch-Doctors, it is recommended that individuals have a strong belief in Fellfang's spirituality.

The Hunt — Separately referred to as hunters.

Together they make up a well-educated group: The Hunt. They have extensive knowledge of Fellfang territory and everything that happens within it. They track the movements of herds, catalogue nesting grounds and section off dangerous areas due to denning predators. They are Fellfang's primary providers and work as a unit to ensure that the balance of the territory is kept together. This means managing overhunting, which requires population control and everything that comes with learning the different and difficult terrains of Fellfang. They are the enforcers of consumption: meaning that they report those who hunt for sport or make poor judgement; such as killing a nursing mother and leaving her calf to die.

Vanguard — The diplomatic scouts of Fellfang.

The guards, the envoys, the messengers. Vanguards fall under many different names yet carry out one similar goal: to protect Fellfang. They are the first defense during an attack and in day-to-day life, deal with matters involving loners, messengers at the border and other threats that fall within Fellfang territory. Because of this they must have a keen sense of judgement to assess the situation. Vanguards are expected to be stealthy, tactful and calm under pressure. Though there is no body required to join the Vanguards it is expected that the individual is healthy and physically capable of travelling long distances. They must have a strong knowledge of the territory and its secrets — as well as other lands beyond the border. It is not unheard of that Vanguards act as an extension of the Jarl, being sent on missions or more often than not: warriors of Fellfang.
