Grayard

Esoteric | Militaristic | Doomsday | Integrity | Humility

"I pledge allegiance to the pack, With honor, humility, and aplomb…"

Grayard. An old pack. Too old to know its origin story. One many assumed was long-dead. Some only wish it were.

They scrape by in a labyrinthian landscape of wood, earth and stone, surviving on close familial bonds and a voracious appetite for loyalty. The world outside of their territory is just as dead to them as they were to it — they didn't need anyone but themselves. At least, that's how it was for many generations…

One evening, following the untimely death of the former Alpha who stepped down from his duties the year prior, The Grayardi were turned loose upon the outside world for their own good. The decedent's daughter and current Alpha, Honorable Bentwood, suspected poison. Murder. And no suspects. Her first decree thereafter was to purge the pack.

Following her father's funeral, all wolves are to be stripped of their roles and ranks, and evicted from the territory — alone — until next Supper, at Darkfall, in four day's time. There shall be no conspiring. No collaborating. No teaming up. A test of individual mettle, of sorts. Those who exhibit loyalty by returning for a fresh start, with honesty in their hearts, will be granted clemency for their sins and their egos, no matter how long their homecoming takes them.

Those who wish to abandon their post for the curiosities of the wilds may. Those who flee in their shame, for their crimes, may also go, disguised in innocence and stealing their freedom to wear as a burden around their heart forever more. And may their knowledge of the impending end of the world go with them.

Grayard and Its Members

Grayard is a combined found-family band and well-coordinated brigade. Pack members are respected as 'siblings,' or 'cousins' to one another. They strive to live their lives with integrity in order to nurture the chaotic force by which they are allowed to even exist: Nature. It is the ultimate lifelong responsibility that they all (must) respect.

Wolves of the pack sing together, snooze together (exceptions allowed,) dine together, and work together to solve problems, of both the physical and personal variety. There are very few secrets kept among the Grayardi, or so it is said, for an individual's business is everyone's business. When one Grayardi is hurting, all Grayardi are hurting. Every Grayard life is equally precious and is to be protected and defended, no matter the wrongs incurred. Forgiveness is granted swiftly. At least, it is to an extent… Punishments are particularly harsh, intended to discourage violations in the first place. In contrast, achievements are lauded and celebrations are frequent.

The pack has a typical hierarchical dynamic, but from the Grayardi perspective, wolves do not serve the wolves above their rank; they serve those below them. The Alpha's duties are to support their Betas and Thetas as best as they can, which in turn supports the rest of the pack members. In Grayard, all wolves are considered equal, but do not share equal responsibility.

Grayard and Outsiders

To an outsider, Grayard appears eerie and uninviting. The pack wolves found here are well-coordinated, typically silent, and generally unsettling to a trespassing stranger caught unaware. When it comes to larger bands of wolves whose motives are unknown, Grayardi are expected to be immediately defensive, standoffish, coy, and even hostile if necessary. When traveling (however rare,) wolves of Grayard speak to outsiders only when spoken to and reveal very little about themselves, but their answers are always honest – usually.

However, individual or small-scale groups of outsiders at the pack's borders are actually something of a novelty to Grayard and are treated as such. Once sighted, strangers are secretly followed in the territory and observed until a best course of action is determined. They may be greeted with optimistic caution or curious suspicion - either way, it is apparent they have been followed for some time and are not about to be turned loose.

Strangers are most commonly presented with a canned question or staged dilemma, known among packmates as a 🐾bough ('bow,') wordplay intended. The bough is a simple test of an outsider's willingness to share information: an honesty check. If passed, the Grayardi will bow their heads, a covert non-verbal signal to others that the outsider in question seems trustworthy. The Grayardi may shed their reservations and put on a more welcoming demeanor. If brought before the Alpha, the Grayardi will bow their head to the Alpha as a signal that the new audience has passed the bough.

Trustworthy newcomers are then welcomed eagerly and made to feel at home. New packmates are treated as honored guests for their first nights in the main den, the Yard. Once they are officially initiated by pledging their fealty to the pack, they are expected to perform pack duties on a regular schedule and learn the pack's ways. The general consensus of the pack is that it is "Us against the World," and covet any opportunity to welcome new members into their fold.

Grayard has a generations-long poor reputation with their neighbors and packs with particularly acute recordkeeping. At an early age, Grayardi learn to be skeptical and suspicious of others as the pack has a storied history of being raided, their lands hunted upon without permission or to excess, or in other cases of being taken advantage of or deeply disrespected by outside packs or roaming bands of loners. Over the generations, the Grayardi have responded by becoming somewhat isolated and militaristic. Grayard is keen on keeping an even playing field when engaging with others. They are known to swap favors and privately tally debts, even amongst themselves. Outsiders not initiated into the pack are not to leave with more than they have earned, and similarly, Grayard is not to dispatch outsiders with less than they deserve.

Culture

Grayard values truth and honesty above all else: a wolf is nothing if they are not honest.

Also, the end of the world is imminent.

Teamwork and humility are the next cornerstones of the pack's culture! Pups and new pack members are encouraged (and sometimes set up) to fail early and often, with an emphasis on building the courage it takes to try again. Wins are celebrated with noisy enthusiasm. At each rank-up, pack members are tested on their knowledge and abilities, but there is an unspoken allowance for leniency if the ranking wolf shows up to their test with the support of their friends and immediate family. Cheating on tests is outwardly frowned upon, but it is known that sometimes we all need a little help (or hints) in life from those closest to us. Rank trials can include the role of Alpha, which is not strictly a hereditary honor. Any wolf of high enough rank may vie for the seat of Alpha, if they dare.

Oh, the 'end of the world' thing? All good things come to an end, and the end of the world - known as The Fell - is no exception. The Grayardi do what they can with this information, living life to the fullest with fun and splendor because any day might be their last. But more importantly, the Grayardi live their most honorable lives with integrity and humility - for it is the honorable state of one's ego upon death that upholds one's right to return to Nature. And the end could come at any time, so you'd better be prepared by living honorably and maintaining a clean ego! If we don't, there may be nothing left to nurture Nature's resurgence! A practice called Admission may salvage an ego, but it is done at the expense of the Alpha's own ego. It is ultimately for the Alpha's sake that pack members strive for integrity and humility. They are the martyr for the pack, and given the apt but tragically ironic prefix Honorable.

To the rest of the world, it seems Grayard is a crude stain of death and ignorance in the Domain, a persistent horde of foolhardy wolves who self-soothe with music and parties, and always seem to bounce back from one mishap or another. But Grayard has its well-kept secrets, choosing to view the false generations-old reputation it garnered for itself as pitiable lies. Grayard's immediate neighboring packs are especially disliked for peddling these rumors. Creatures who repeat and feed those lies are not worthy of the truth, so Grayard's secrets remain locked behind humble lips. Even its name is cheekily deceptive - "Gray yard," or perhaps a long, lost lexical shift of "Graveyard," implies a dull, uninhabitable wasteland, which it appears to be at some of its borders. Grayard, however, is secretly as wild and vivacious as its inhabitants.

The Grayardi typically have a healthy respect (some call it fear) of water, and as a result are poor swimmers. Many Grayardi have lost their lives to the rapids and rock walls upstream in particular. Dying in water is considered a failure to feed the land, no matter how humble the ego. The bends in the river are named after Grayardi wolves who have drowned there. This practice is not to honor the dead; nothing keeps a Grayard wolf farther away from a bend in the river than naming it after someone they personally knew. Crossing the river is safest downstream, though no one will bat an eye if a wolf crosses one of the many natural land bridges or narrow jump-crossings high above the water. The treacherous terrain may be intimidating to an outsider, but it is natural to a sure-footed Grayardi — though accidents do happen. Their general shortsightedness of potential danger in their surroundings have contributed a number of early deaths throughout their generations.

Grayard has some historical figures passed down by name throughout their culture, but one previous Alpha stands out as a figurehead among the rest: the Late Honorable Gray Rosewood. It is understood that Rosewood spearheaded the pack's philosophy of rising above and turning the other cheek to the slanderous reputation of their pack. In simple terms, Rosewood is the founder of Grayard's humility.

Rules of Laws & Consequences

No wolf outranks Grayard Law. These laws go back generations. The general sentiment of Grayard's rules can be summarized by the Late Honorable Gray Rosewood's words, which have become a mantra of the pack: "Be good; if you cannot be good, be careful." (It is still hotly debated among the contemporary Catch members if this was intended as a tongue-in-cheek statement.)

Of the Pack

  • Respect all packmates equally, regardless of age, branch, rank or tenure.
  • Intentionally bringing harm to another Grayardi will result in punishment.
  • For their safety, pups may not leave the Yard; juveniles and visitors leaving the Yard must be accompanied by a wolf of Delta rank or higher.
  • Ceremonies and celebrations are paramount to pack culture; unexcused absenteeism is an affront to those honored. Punishment will follow.
  • Before one starts learning to bough, one must first Pledge themselves to the pack.
  • Thews and the Yard Warden have final say in all matters brought before the Free Council with a two-thirds majority.
  • The Honorable Alpha has final say in all matters brought before them.
  • Wolves born in Grayard will carry the first name 'Gray.'
  • Pledged wolves who defect will be stripped of their rank, title, and the name Gray if they carry it. (Wolves who departed during Bentwood's cleansing may return at any time; if previously carried, the name Gray will be returned to them.)

Of the Land

  • Respect Nature as your packmate.
  • Destruction of landmarks will result in punishment.
  • Initiating the end of the world on purpose will result in punishment.
  • Wasting prey or other resources will result in punishment.
  • Swimming and fishing in the upper ten bends of the Wash is forbidden. Swimmers and fishers do so at their own risk; those who drown will not be laid to rest with a Night-Howl.
    • Swimming in this location is forbidden, but not punishable.
    • Rosewood's Amendment: The drowned may be memorialized quietly by the immediate family. The pack will fast for one day cycle in atonement.

Of Outsiders

  • Respect for outsiders is earned.
  • Unpledged wolves may not bough outsiders.
  • Pack members inviting an outside party into the Yard or territory without first boughing them are equally responsible for their guest's behavior. Any punishment incurred by a guest will be shared by their host.
  • Pack members may not leave the territory, with the exception of impeding and pursuing enemies and thieves for punishment.
  • Non-diplomatic visitors ("Scabs") residing within Grayard's territory owe back what they have been given.

Criminal sentencing, punishments, and consequences may only be administered in the name of retributive justice by the Yard Warden, Free Council, or Honorable Alpha.

Grayard has some additional… expectations of its pack wolves that are not official laws or amendments, and are largely unknown by most pack members. Only the highly keen and long-lived may pick up on such details. They are typically upheld only by the Free Council, the Yard Warden, and the Alpha themselves:

In Secrecy

  • Wolves who miss Supper without excuse, especially if more than once, will be regarded with suspicion and closely but secretly monitored (unless delayed or hampered beyond their control, as in any case of emergency.)
  • Unless it is part of their duty as a Rake, wolves should not be encouraged to learn to swim. No wolves shall teach each other the art of swimming unless they are learning as a Rake.
  • Any pledged wolf who willingly abandons Grayard is not only a defector, but a dishonorable traitor. They should not be welcomed back, and they should not be treated as common outsiders; they should be considered an enemy of the pack. Encounters at the border may result in a skirmish, otherwise they will be taken straight to the Alpha (sans bough,) to be dealt with. Depending on the knowledge with which they abscond, they may be hunted down and "punished" by the Yard Warden - a rare case.
    • An unknown number of wolves have been declared "drowned" in this way.
  • Known to Ribs & higher: Visitors and "Scabs" may not come and go as they please, and not just for their safety.
    • Visitors may not leave the Yard unaccompanied, and may not leave the sightline of their chaperone anywhere in the territory.
    • Scabs may be followed in secrecy.
    • Should a Visitor or Scab wish to leave the territory, they will be escorted to the border by a chaperone, and will be secretly flanked and followed by any available Gophers and/or Watchers.
  • Once an unpledged wolf departs, they must be kept outside of the territory's borders. Watcher patrols will be heightened for at least four days.
  • As Scabs are treated as guests in Grayard the same as honored Visitors, the longer a Scab stays in the Yard unpledged, the greater their debt to the pack grows…

Branches (Tasks)

Gopher > Gophers — Gatherers, small-game/solo hunters, scouts

Gophers alert the pack of intruders. They are expected to know the layout and secrets of the territory better than any. Gophers are solo workers and will report to the Yard Warden, if instated.

Watcher > Watchers — Offensive guards, territory patrol, large-game/group hunt leads

Watchers are most often facing down strangers and are expected to have the strongest bough. They will chase out the unwelcome and escort the defenseless. Watchers may tag-team their work and will report to the Yard Warden, if instated.

Rake > Rakes — Defenders of the inner homestead, engineers, hunt assistance

Rakes are the Grayard's fiercest fighters, Rosewood forbid they should need to put their skills to use. Rakes are most often assessing and evaluating the safety and structural integrity of common routes throughout the territory. Rakes are the only wolves in Grayard encouraged to learn to swim, at their own risk. Rakes work most typically in a singular team, but may sometimes divide work if their numbers are great enough, and will report to the Yard Warden, if instated.

Swift > Swifts — Caretakers/healers, mediators, celebrations, song-leaders

Swifts stay closer to the Yard, tending to the sick, injured, young and old in the infirmary. Should squabbles arise that cannot be resolved on their own, wolves are expected to seek the help of a Swift before thinking of bringing their issue to the Free Council or Alpha.

Catch > Catch — Tradition, ceremonies, history, knowledge, planning

The Catch have the arduous task of maintaining Grayard's history and future planning, and passing it onto the next generation. They also practice and perform the pack's many ceremonies. The Catch keep tabs on all wolves and their progress in Grayard - for the end of the world, of course.

The Pledge

Initiation for all pack members involves swearing their fealty to Grayard in front of the Alpha and at least one additional witness with the following recited Pledge. Juveniles will typically be sworn in at Supper. Once sworn in, the Quick may free-roam throughout the territory. Younger juveniles should stick with a friend or guardian if intending to cross The Wash, or going into the Prickly Woods.

*The Grayard Pledge does not have to be a canonical piece submitted to the group, but it can be! Let the Pack Leader know when you want your wolf to be considered Pledged so they may begin to work toward picking up a task.
