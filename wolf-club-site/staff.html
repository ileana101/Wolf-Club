Staff Code of Conduct

Domain of the Wolf is a group of individuals passionate about solo and collaborative storytelling through artwork, prose, roleplays, and character development and management. The individuals who make up the group's community are a constant ebb and flow, but the intrinsic, core values remain the same:

Be Safe & Have Fun.

Maintaining a safe environment for our community is paramount to fostering open dialogue and the freedom of expression. We're all here to have fun, drawing and writing dogs* together without feeling overburdened by bloated requirements or social pressures!

(*k it's wolves, my dudes)

The group is run by a small team of voluntary staff that ebbs and flows with its community, who operate different positions to keep the group and its platforms (deviantart Group, website, Discord,) running smoothly. It goes without saying, but the staff of DoTW are looked to for everything, and we should always remember that we are looked up to and that our behavior is a reflection of what is expected of our members. To become a member of DoTW staff, all members must read and abide by the staff team's Code of Conduct.

1. A level of professionalism & integrity is expected of all staff members and how they conduct themselves for the duration of their tenure on the team.

2. Be respectful of members and each other, both in and outside of official DoTW spaces.

No one should be spoken to or about with the intent to shame or belittle; nor should anyone be spoken of without the same respect as if they were present, including in private conversations/spaces with former, future, or current members. Staff discovered dragging other staff members, regular members, or characters will be reconsidered in their position on the staff team.

3. Be kind when and where you can afford it.

Your actions and words carry more weight in your position, including among your fellow staff members; wield that weight wisely. When brainstorming, discussing, drafting, and replying to messages, your input should not be at the expense of someone else's participation.

4. Communicate clearly and in a timely manner to one another and to members.

Over-communicate where possible, including asking questions to tease out more information. "I don't know," is a complete answer, but "I don't know, I can get back to you tomorrow" is even better, but only if you follow through on that promise.

5. Listen to what others have to say, especially when discussions have a lot of differing opinions.

No one's opinion is any more or any less valid than anyone else's, and no one should be made to feel so.

6. Uphold humility in your position.

You are not on staff because you 'can do it better,' you are on staff because you were driven to go the extra mile to maintain the group as a fun and safe space to play and create together as a community. At the end of the day, you are still a member of this group.

7. Champion equality among all group members, including fellow staff.

Group rules must be applied to all members equally. Staff does not indulge in exceptions, benefits, or luxuries for themselves, their friends, or each other. Being on staff puts you in a position of knowledge; do not use that knowledge for your or your friends' personal gains. This responsibility includes encouraging others in areas outside of your immediate interest/benefit.

8. Be active, present, and available for your teammates and the community outside of your regular IC content production.

Staff should be available ideally for at least 1 hour each day or combined hours on select days (eg. 2 hours Tuesdays, 2 hours Thursday, 3 hours across weekend.) to check in on staff discussions and perform duties. Checking in only once or a few times a week could become problematic for your teammates. Depending on your role, your extended absence may hold up critical staff or group action! It is vital that you participate in debates and discussions when available. This ensures that we're balancing the amount of work among our admins and helps us to make well-rounded decisions for the group.

Being on staff inherently requires added work on top of the minimum group participation, and while everyone has different comfort levels of activity (IC and OOC), certain staff are held to a higher standard of activity than regular members.

  • Council members need to make themselves available in case of serious discussion at any time.
  • Mods should be actively curating the health of the community quickly and efficiently.
  • Pack Leaders should ideally be posting content more than the standard minimum group requirement, in addition to creating content opportunities for their pack members.
  • Scribes should avail themselves to their fellow pack members and their Pack Leader on a regular basis.
  • All roles are able to moderate the Discord server in some capacity; this does not necessarily mean actively participating in discussion, but being seen makes your presence known.

9. Take breaks when you need them.

Walk away from a heated discussion if you cannot participate with that above code of conduct in mind. Don't only take a hiatus when you feel overly pressured to perform; say something to at least one fellow staff member so they can help offload your duties to relieve your burden! There is no shame in letting work go into another party's hands, especially if it helps lighten your mental load. Ghosting the team will only add mounting pressure to everyone else in the long run.

10. If you see something, say something.

Take initiative if a member or another member of staff is causing concern; bring it up with the appropriate Mod(s) to inform them.

11. Have discretion for the debates, member complaints, reprimands and other group records that may come up in staff discussions

Information may inherently circle around the member base from affected members (as is their right to discuss things amongst their friends and RP partners) but staff conversations should remain private and should not be disclosed in any format without explicit permission from the involved member(s). Staff discovered circulating screenshots or synopsis of private conversations with uninvolved parties without involved members' permission will be reconsidered in their position on the staff team.

If a member informs staff of private knowledge they have been given by another staff member, that member shall in no way be 'retaliated against,' and it will have no standing in considering them for future events, plots, promotions, etc.

12. Leave your position mindful of what you are leaving behind.

Should your tenure on staff come to an end for any reason, whether your departure comes as a surprise or was planned, allow yourself time to relinquish your duties so the group may continue to carry them. Please pass along all relevant documentation or pending responsibilities to the remaining staff in your role or in another position. Communicate pending projects to a trusted fellow staff member. Your transition out of your role has a ripple effect on the member base; the smoother it is, the less it will be felt by the rest of the group.

If you are not just stepping down from your position but also leaving the group, know that your contributions matter.

Staff should never weaponize departures, including their own, by emotionally blackmail or catalyze action and change they wish to see within the group.

Please do not muse or waffle about maybe stepping down from your role in public DoTW spaces as it causes distress and uncertainty among the member base at large. If you need to talk to someone to help work through your decisions, please do so only in private spaces (including official Staff channels.)

13. Staff shall not take another member's departure from the team as an opportunity to openly vent or complain, or make light jabs or remarks of the departed staff member's performance or tenure

Again, our behavior is a reflection of what is expected of our members – even when members are not present. Former staff may of course be mentioned in reference to ongoing discussions, but should be done so neutrally. Support for each other and members should be conducted without reaching for the expense of members/staff of the past. We may all hold our opinions, but they should not inform our conduct or color our language. See #2.

Staff Strike System

As humans, we are all capable of making mistakes or not being our best selves. One mistake should not eject you from your role, nor should "mistakes" be overlooked for so long that quiet unrest builds up in the member base or in staff channels.

In the event that a concern for a member's or staff member's behavior warrants intervention and oversight by Mods and/or the Council, a 3-Strike plan will be implemented to track the member's or staff member's progress over time at the Mods'/Council's discretion.

  • Strikes are to be issued to the staff member in question via private Discord threads or Notes, never behind a bot or anonymous entity.
  • If someone must receive multiple strikes in a short period of time, they should not all come from the same person to prevent a staff/member from feeling targeted by a specific individual.
  • Strikes are to be tracked privately by Mods/Council in a closed Discord channel for clean recordkeeping. Receipts (screenshots/thread link) of the issuance are required.
  • Strikes may be removed or fall off over 3 months of time.

In addition to Strikes, depending on the severity of the incident, the staff/member may incur additional discouragements, such as:

  • Temporary ban from in-character events.
  • Temporary ban from certain Discord channels (with a special "snooze" role to remove their access to suspended channels.)
  • Loss of the month's bones or forfeiture of the next month's bones (activity must still be performed as normal unless the staff/member should incur a red dot.)
  • Other discouragements not listed as approved by Mods/Council.
